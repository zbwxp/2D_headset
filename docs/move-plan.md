## 1. The module, `move/`

- **What it does:** turns input into a displacement for one *session*: a mouse drag (press → moves → release) or a keyboard run (arrow keys pressed and held, released). It owns only the input's timing and amounts: start, preview, commit, cancel, and the step sizes. It knows nothing of lines or pictures and imports nothing.
- **A target** is what a caller hands it: `begin() → boolean` (may refuse: nothing to move, locked, a mode that writes nothing), `preview(offset)`, `commit(offset)` (one write, one undo step), `cancel()`. What moves, whether it may, and how it is undone stay with the target (dot 1791733234).
- **Mouse:** the offset is the pointer's displacement since the press; commit on release, nothing committed for a release where it was pressed.
- **Keyboard:** an arrow key starts a session (if none runs) and adds a step in its direction; a held key's repeats add more; other arrows join the same session. **Steps:** 1 drawing unit; **Shift** ×10; **Ctrl** ×0.1 (bowen). The session commits **once**, when the last arrow is released — so holding an arrow is one undoable move (dot 1791733234).
- **One session at a time** (dot 1791733296): while a mouse drag runs, arrow keys are ignored; while an arrow session runs, a press of the canvas is ignored (as a second pointer is). **Esc, leaving the window, a tool change, a view change, playback, Open and unmount all cancel** the running session, keyboard or mouse alike (the target's `cancel`: nothing written) — the bench gives a session its one gesture slot and its one cancel path. A key release arriving after a cancel, or after the target has changed (another drawing, another picture), writes nothing.
- **Session rules** (dot 1791733489):
  - **What `begin` captures:** the drawing, the view, the target (e.g. the selection's units, the picture as drawn) and the start position. The preview is always start + the accumulated offset; `commit` first checks the drawing, view and target are still those — otherwise it is a cancel. The selection is taken at `begin`, never read again at commit.
  - **Undo / redo during a key run** end the run and write nothing; nothing earlier is undone with them (the run was never committed). During a mouse drag they wait, as before.
  - **What ends a session (cancel):** besides Esc, leaving the window, tool, view, playback, Open and unmount — an undo or redo, a change of the selection or of the layer scope, the target replaced or deleted, and focus moving inside the page into a field or a slider. A key release after a cancel writes nothing; and after a cancel, a held key's repeats (`repeat: true`) never start a new session — a real new press is needed.
  - **Nothing written until the end:** a session's preview never touches the drawing, its history or autosave (core's `edit` is one synchronous transaction; no callback is kept open across key events). One commit at the end; a run that comes back to its start (right then left) commits nothing. The history a click's own selection change makes is its own, not merged into the move.
  - **Ending before writing:** the session ends (state cleared, listeners gone) *before* its one commit, so a commit's notification cannot re-enter it or commit twice; a commit refused or throwing still leaves it ended, and the refusal is shown as any refusal is.
- **Ctrl, Shift and the shortcuts** (dot 1791733489): the bench today folds Ctrl into ⌘ (`meta`), so Ctrl + arrow would go down the shortcut path. The move input reads **Ctrl on its own** and **Option (Alt)**: **Option (Alt) + arrow** is the slow move (bowen 1791733775), and Ctrl + arrow too, while ⌘ / Ctrl + Z, C, X, V stay what they are (no arrow involved). With **Shift and Ctrl together** the factors multiply: ×10 × ×0.1 = a 1-unit step — stated, not a special case.
- **Keys that stay elsewhere** (dot 1791733296): a text field, an input method and a slider keep their own arrow keys (the bench's typing check, and a focused range input, come first); the step sizes apply to moving only — Shift keeps its meaning of adding to a selection when clicking.

## 2. Wiring

- **The selection (A / V):** what a drag or the keys move is exactly the selection (a marquee over some lines moves those lines; it never grows into their whole connected curves; dot 1791733296). Interaction's drag of the selection becomes a `move/` session, and interaction takes arrow keys for the selection through the same session: the target's `begin` takes the scoped selection's units, `preview` shows the ghost (as today's drag preview), `commit` is one `translate(dx, dy, units)`. Arrow keys move the selection only with A or V and a selection; otherwise they do what they do now (nothing on the canvas).
- **The background picture (Move picture on):** today's drag and arrow keys become `move/` sessions with a target that previews the picture and commits one `setReference(id, { x, y })`; held arrows become one undo step (today each press is one). Its binding to the picture as drawn and its checks stay as they are (`RefBind`, §8a).
- **Boundary — a decision for dot:** interaction may import only core's package root (interaction test 2). Proposed: allow `move/` too — it imports nothing and knows nothing of core, so interaction still depends on no other app part. The alternative, the bench owning the selection's drag and keys, would move a decided part of interaction out of it; not proposed.

- **Playback** (dot 1791733296): a move in playback stays a correction (`correct`), with its own picking, locks and pose rules; this package does not route playback through `move/` and never turns a playback move into `translate`. Arrow keys in playback do nothing in this package; the keyboard correction is a requested later stage (below), with `correct` as the target.
